Why Plume

It opens the file. That is the whole trick.

Every Markdown app wants to own your notes first and show them second. Plume opens the file you clicked, renders it properly, and gets out of the way.

Opens with one double-click

Plume registers itself for .md, .markdown, .mkd and five more extensions. A second file opens in a new window of the running app — no cold start.

Edits when you want it to

Press the pencil and the document becomes editable — the raw Markdown, nothing between what you type and what is saved. Press it again and you are reading.

Reads Obsidian notes properly

Wiki links, embeds and section transclusion, callouts, ==highlights==, #tags, block ids and front-matter Properties — all rendered the way Obsidian does it.

Full GitHub-flavoured Markdown

Tables, task lists, footnotes, definition lists, emoji shortcodes, syntax highlighting, KaTeX math and Mermaid diagrams — rendered, not escaped.

Live reload that keeps your place

Save the file in any editor and Plume updates in place — same scroll position, same spot on the page. Write in one window, read in the other.

Safe with files you did not write

Documents are sanitised, scripts never run, and links never launch programs. A note cannot make Plume pull files off other machines on your network.

Screenshots

Light or dark, it reads like a book.

Set the theme, the text size, the reading width and the typeface once. Plume remembers.

Editing

A reader first. An editor when you ask.

Most of the time you want to read. When you want to fix a line, press the pencil — or Ctrl+E — and the raw Markdown is right there. There is no hidden formatting model: what you type is what gets written.

  • Nothing is written until you ask. Typing changes nothing on disk.
  • Unsaved work is never dropped quietly. A dot marks it, and leaving asks first.
  • A file changed elsewhere cannot overwrite you. Plume stops and lets you choose.
  • Tab indents, Enter continues a list, Ctrl+S saves, Esc goes back to reading.
Syncing

Pick a folder. Forget about it.

Choose one folder on your computer and Plume keeps everything in it — notes, images and sub-folders — in your vault, by itself. It never moves or renames anything: your folder stays exactly where it is.

Your folders are kept

Projects/Plume.md arrives as Projects/Plume.md. Two notes called README in different folders never collide.

Documents and images only

Markdown, text and pictures go up. Programs, installers and archives are refused by an allow-list and stay on your computer.

Nothing is flattened

If a document changed on another machine, Plume refuses the upload and asks which copy to keep. It can save both.

For Claude Code and Codex

Give your assistant a memory that outlives the session.

Plume Vault ships an MCP server. Create a token, point your assistant at it, and it can keep project decisions, notes and running logs somewhere that is still there next week — and that you can open in Plume on any of your machines.

  • Eight tools — list, read, write, append, search and delete documents, plus the graph and storage status.
  • Plain Markdown. Nothing proprietary — the notes are files you can read, edit and take away.
  • A token, not your password. Revoke it in one click and the access is gone.

For Obsidian users

Your vault, without opening the vault.

Share a note with someone who does not run Obsidian, or just read one note without waiting for a whole workspace to load. Plume speaks the same dialect.

  • Wiki links — [[note]], [[Note#Heading|alias]], and they resolve across the vault.
  • Embeds and transclusion — ![[image.png|300]] and ![[Note#Section]].
  • Callouts — > [!tip], including the foldable > [!warning]- kind.
  • Properties from front matter, #tags, %%comments%% and ^block ids.
  • Open in Obsidian for vault files, when you do want the full thing.
Everything else

The small things you expect.

Find in page

Ctrl+F, with next and previous and a match count — the way a browser does it.

Back and forward

Follow a link between notes and walk back out, with Alt+← or your mouse side buttons.

Print and export to PDF

On white paper, with a real PDF outline built from your headings.

Open in your editor

Jump into VS Code or Cursor if you have them, or the system text editor. Ctrl+Shift+E.

Updates that ask first

Plume tells you when a new version is out and installs it when you say so. Every download is checked against the release's published checksum.
